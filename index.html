Each project starts with a real question and ends with something a decision-maker can use.

Where is Nyanga’s mountain vegetation changing, and why?

ProblemManagers lacked a clear picture of where vegetation is shifting, what drives it and what comes next.
ApproachBuilt a Google Earth Engine pipeline on harmonised Landsat imagery (2003–2023). Mapped trends and hotspots, used Random Forest to attribute drivers, and projected vulnerability under four climate pathways.
OutcomeMaps and evidence showing where change is happening, what is behind it and which areas are most at risk.
Google Earth EngineLandsatRandom ForestCMIP6 scenariosHonours dissertation

ProblemFarmers need timely, reliable weather and crop information to plan.
ApproachAs GIS intern in Agro-meteorology, took synoptic observations, captured and quality-checked station data, and monitored rainfall onset, dry spells, soil moisture and crop conditions.
OutcomeClean, checked data feeding agro-meteorological bulletins and farmer advisories.
Climate dataQuality controlCrop monitoringMeteorological Services Dept

Is the University of Zimbabwe campus losing its green space?

ProblemCampus growth was assumed to be eating into green space, with no measurement of how much or where.
ApproachClassified dry-season Sentinel-2 imagery (2018 vs 2024) with k-means on NDVI, NDBI, NDWI and brightness, then ran transition, fragmentation and hotspot analysis.
OutcomeA quantified, mapped view of land-cover change that supports campus planning.
Sentinel-2k-meansChange detectionPython
