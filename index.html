Nyanga Vegetation Decision Support System · Flagship / BSc Hons Dissertation

Nyanga National Park, Zimbabwe · 2003–2023 · Supervisor: Ms. K. Chirenje · Google Earth Engine + Python
kNDVIEVINDWIRandom ForestSSP126–585GEE JavaScript
Problem

Park managers in rugged montane terrain have no objective, spatially explicit way to know where vegetation is degrading, why, or what it will look like under future climate. Decisions are made on patrol anecdote, and standard NDVI saturates and is distorted by topographic shadow on Nyanga's steep slopes.

Solution

An automated 21-year monitoring and projection engine with a click-and-go interface:

  • Harmonised Landsat 5/7/8/9 + Sentinel-2 archive with cloud/shadow masking and atmospheric correction
  • Non-linear kNDVI (plus EVI, NDWI) to defeat saturation and terrain-shadow bias
  • Theil–Sen / Mann-Kendall trend surfaces classified into greening, stable and browning zones
  • Random Forest driver model over 20+ topo-climatic predictors (elevation, slope, aspect, TWI, rainfall, VPD, temperature, fire, night-lights, distance-to-road) with train/test validation (R², RMSE, MAE, bias)
  • Forward projections under SSP1-2.6, 2-4.5, 3-7.0, 5-8.5 to 2063, with Monte-Carlo uncertainty and CO₂-fertilisation logic
  • Guided 4-step decision flow: pick a question → pick a year → click a hotspot → get ranked management actions (prescribed burn, restoration, drought mitigation, erosion control), with a baseline-vs-future comparison toggle
Economic Value — how it earns

Replaces a recurring, expensive consultancy with a reusable tool.

  • Cost avoidance: a comparable 20-year degradation baseline study is normally outsourced; this runs free on Earth Engine and repeats in minutes.
  • Targeted spend: restoration and fire budgets are directed only at the ~14% browning area instead of the whole park — a direct reduction in field cost per hectare of impact.
  • Sellable product: the same engine re-points at any protected area, catchment, farm block or mining concession — a licensable SaaS / per-site monitoring subscription.
  • Unlocks funding: quantified, verifiable trend and carbon-relevant vegetation evidence is exactly what donors, REDD+/carbon-credit verifiers and ESG auditors require before disbursing.
Revenue model: per-site monitoring subscription + baseline/verification reporting for carbon & ESG.

Zimbabwe Real-Time Weather & Asset Risk Dashboard

Web GIS · Leaflet + Turf.js + Chart.js · 24-hour rolling meteorological analysis
LeafletTurf.jsHeatmapsShapefile uploadChart.js
Problem

Weather data reaches operators as raw numbers on separate websites. Nobody can see, on one screen, which of my assets, fields or crews is currently exposed to heat, wind, cloud or rainfall — so response is reactive and losses are absorbed after the fact.

Solution

An asset-centric environmental risk engine in the browser — no install, no GIS licence:

  • Live station network with clustering, wind barbs, temperature/cloud/precipitation heat layers
  • 24-hour time slider with play animation for temperature (current / min / max / mean)
  • Turf.js spatial intersection: drag in your own shapefile ZIP (read client-side with shpjs) and the dashboard reports which of your polygons fall inside a hazard zone
  • Hotspot and correlation analysis tools + station time-series charts and summary KPI cards
  • Built-in legend and user manual so a non-GIS operator can run it unaided
Economic Value — how it earns
  • Loss prevention: early frost/heat/storm warnings let farms and mines protect crops, livestock and outdoor crews — every avoided incident is direct saved cost.
  • Insurance & agri-finance: station-verified weather histories underpin index-based insurance pricing and claims validation — a paid data service.
  • Subscription SaaS: tiered per-asset monthly pricing for farms, logistics, mining and event operators; enterprise tier adds custom shapefile asset registers and alerting.
  • Zero infrastructure cost: fully client-side, hosts free on GitHub Pages — margin is almost entirely retained.
Revenue model: SaaS per-asset subscription + weather-risk data licensing to insurers.

YOLOv8 Cabbage Field Detector (Desktop GIS App)

Python · Ultralytics YOLOv8 · Rasterio + GeoPandas · CustomTkinter GUI
YOLOv8OpenCVRasterioGeoPandasShapefile export
Problem

Counting crops and estimating plant population from drone or satellite imagery is done by hand — slow, subjective and impossible at scale. Agronomists need plant counts to forecast yield, but manual counting of a single field can take hours.

Solution

A one-window desktop application that any agronomist can run:

  • Load a trained YOLOv8 .pt model and a GeoTIFF — press run
  • Object detection over the raster with OpenCV pre-processing and confidence control
  • Pixel detections converted to real-world geometry via the raster affine transform (Rasterio) and written out as a georeferenced shapefile (GeoPandas + Shapely)
  • Total detection count, annotated preview image and Matplotlib summary rendered inside the GUI
  • Complex CV/geospatial back-end hidden behind four buttons — no Python knowledge needed
Economic Value — how it earns
  • Yield forecasting: accurate plant counts drive harvest, labour and transport planning — directly reducing wastage and overtime.
  • Priced per hectare: drone survey + automated count is a repeatable service billed per hectare per season, with near-zero marginal cost per extra field.
  • Replaces days of manual scouting with minutes of compute — the labour differential is the margin.
  • Retrainable: the same pipeline works for maize, tobacco, orchards, livestock or infrastructure assets — one codebase, many markets.
Revenue model: per-hectare crop-counting service + model retraining for new crops.

Field-tested: 613 cabbages counted from a single drone pass, each with a georeferenced bounding box.

Landscape Pattern & Scale-Effect Analysis

Spatial ecology · Grain (4–40 m) × extent sensitivity · Python + FRAGSTATS-style metrics
Class AreaPatch densityEdge densityFragmentation
Problem

Habitat-fragmentation numbers quoted in reports change dramatically with the pixel size and study extent used — so two analysts can reach opposite conservation conclusions from the same landscape. Nobody quantifies that uncertainty.

Solution

A systematic grain-and-extent sensitivity study: class area, number of patches, mean patch size and edge density computed across six grain sizes and multiple extents, with 28 charts, data tables and a written interpretation of each scaling response, delivered as a reproducible report.

Economic Value — how it earns
  • Defensible EIA reports: fragmentation findings that survive regulator and legal challenge — the core deliverable of paid environmental impact assessment work.
  • Avoids costly rework: choosing the correct resolution up front prevents a rejected assessment and a repeat survey.
  • Methodology IP: the reusable scaling workflow becomes a standard template billed on every subsequent EIA or biodiversity-offset contract.
Revenue model: EIA / biodiversity-offset consulting deliverable.

GNSS Field Survey & Geodatabase Design

GPS/GNSS capture · Topology validation · PostGIS / File geodatabase
GNSSPostGISTopologyQA/QC
Problem

Organisations hold scattered, duplicated and mis-projected spatial files with overlapping boundaries and no single source of truth, which causes disputes over land, assets and infrastructure position.

Solution

Field GNSS capture to a designed geodatabase: consistent CRS (UTM 36S / EPSG:32736), enforced domains and relationships, topology rules to remove gaps, slivers and overlaps, and documented metadata so the dataset is auditable.

Economic Value — how it earns
  • Prevents boundary and asset disputes — legal costs avoided far exceed the survey fee.
  • Billable per-point / per-day survey work with a geodatabase build-out as an added-value deliverable.
  • Enables every downstream product: clean geometry is the precondition for the dashboards, models and detection services above.
Revenue model: day-rate survey + fixed-fee geodatabase design.
